# Generate Directions

Generate exactly three directions after product analysis. Their differences must be structural and strategic, not three palettes applied to the same layout.

## Direction contract

Use the same fields for A, B, and C:

```text
Name
Thesis — one sentence connecting the direction to product purpose
Personality — 3–5 attributes
Visual language — hierarchy, density, composition, imagery
Typography — role and character, not an unverified font dependency
Color philosophy — roles and emotional effect, not arbitrary swatches
Shape language — radius, borders, shadows, grouping
Motion — purpose, intensity, and indicative timing
Component characteristics — navigation, controls, content, data, and states
Research keywords — semantic phrases tied to the direction
Advantages
Risks
Product fit — heuristic plus explanation
Audience fit — heuristic plus explanation
Usability fit — heuristic plus explanation
Distinctiveness — heuristic plus explanation
Implementation complexity — Low, Medium, or High with cause
```

Percentages or scores are optional. If used, call them directional heuristics and make the explanation more prominent than the number.

## Differentiation test

Before presenting, compare all three across:

| Axis | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Hierarchy | Do they prioritize information differently? |
| Density | Do they make meaningfully different density choices? |
| Typography | Does type play a different product role? |
| Color | Does each use color for a different strategic purpose? |
| Shape | Are grouping and geometry materially different? |
| Motion | Does interaction feedback express a different personality? |
| Component strategy | Would navigation, content, and states be composed differently? |

If two options differ mainly by color, merge or replace one. Keep all three plausible: one recommendation and two credible alternatives, not a winner plus caricatures.

## Product specificity

Derive each direction from audience, jobs, trust, density, and emotional needs. Category is context, not a template. A finance product can be calm, editorial, or motivating; a developer tool can be sparse or dense depending on its jobs.

Do not default to generic AI-SaaS traits such as gradient heroes, interchangeable card grids, glass panels, excessive pills, or large decorative headings unless the product evidence gives them a clear job.

## Recommendation

Rank the three directions and recommend one. Explain:

1. Which product tension it resolves best.
2. What it deliberately preserves from the existing UI.
3. Why its main risk is acceptable or how it will be controlled.
4. Why the other two remain legitimate alternatives.

## Selection checkpoint

End with a compact comparison and ask the user to select A, B, or C, or request a rethink. Stop there.

Do not search for components, write `DESIGN.md`, choose on the user's behalf, or edit the application—even when the user says to hurry or “pick whatever looks best.” Delegated taste is permission to recommend, not permission to cross the selection gate.
